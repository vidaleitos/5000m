# Strength and general physical preparation for a distance runner

## The rule that decides everything: general is fine, unspecialisable is not

The athlete's own objection is correct — *"general physical qualities also need developing, it is just
gradual specialisation."* General preparation is real periodisation, not filler.

The error is never "being general". It is prescribing general work that **cannot be specialised
later**:

```
cul-de-sac    bodyweight -> 20 reps -> 2 kg dumbbell        (the end of the road)
real base     bodyweight -> loaded backpack -> 5-6 reps -> more load -> stronger
```

A 20-rep set trains local endurance. Nothing done to "20 reps" turns it into a strength stimulus —
the exercise has to change. So the circuit is not a worse base, it is a base that leads nowhere.

**Test to apply**: *"what does the next progression of this exercise look like?"*
If the answer is "the same thing with more reps", it is not a base.

## Maintenance vs development — believe the stated goal

When the athlete says the purpose is **keeping** strength while mileage rises, size the dose to that,
not to the textbook development dose:

| frequency | effect |
|---|---|
| **1x/week** (real intensity) | maintains most acquired strength |
| 2x/week | maintains + slow gain |
**Never applies per system when there are two systems.** A "2-3x/week" frequency rule taken from a
video and applied to *both* a strength routine and a core routine becomes **six** non-running sessions
a week (~2.5 h) — which is how a maintenance goal silently turns into a second training load.

**When the week already holds three hard running days, 2x/week is the development dose** — 3x does not
fit. Make the two differ in **kind**, not just in number:

| | full session (A) | reduced session (B) |
|---|---|---|
| when | the one fresh morning, >=48 h from hard days | **the evening of the hardest day, after it** |
| content | plyo first, then loaded single-leg, eccentric, push-ups | loaded single-leg + eccentric + band upper back |
| plyo | yes, 20-42 ground contacts | **no** — jump quality after a hard session is zero |
| sets | full | roughly half |

**A lift on the morning after the hardest session is the worst slot in the week**: DOMS peaks 24-48 h
later and lands on the next session. The same work moved to that evening costs nothing — the following
day is a recovery day anyway, so the DOMS is free.

## The time-budget arithmetic that ends the "strength or miles" argument

```
87 km  ~= 7.4 h/week      105 km ~= 8.9 h/week      -> needs +1.5 h

non-running at development dose  ~= 2.5 h/week
non-running at maintenance dose  ~= 1.1 h/week      -> frees +1.4 h
```

**The freed time almost exactly equals the volume increase.** The athlete never had to choose between
strength and mileage — the *dose* was the constraint, not the clock.

Say it as: *"you are not training too much, you are spending a development dose on a maintenance
goal."*

## What actually transfers

| priority | content | dose | why |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | heavy load, low reps (3-6 @ 80-90 % 1RM) | 2x/week | the economy adaptation lives here |
| 2 | plyometrics — **horizontal first** (bounding, single-leg hops, standing long jump) | 2x/week, 20-42 ground contacts | second-strongest; bodyweight is enough |
| 3 | tendon loading — isometrics, eccentrics, slow heavy | inside 1 and 2 | stiffness + injury resilience |
| 4 | trunk anti-rotation / anti-extension | 10-12 min, 2-3x/week, **separate budget** | see below |
| x | hypertrophy sets (8-12 to failure) | — | mass without economy |
| x | high-rep circuits (15-20 x 3) | — | becomes local endurance |

### Zero transfer — say it plainly, with the mechanism

- **Push-ups.** 40-50 per session buys a runner nothing: no running action uses chest/triceps force
  and economy does not depend on it. In one real routine this was roughly **a third of the strength
  time**. Allowed as 2x10 maintenance, never as a programme.
- **Leg-raise families, crunches, bicycle kicks, Russian twists.** These train trunk *flexion*
  endurance. Running's trunk demand is **anti-rotation and anti-extension** — a different direction,
  so volume here does not accumulate toward the thing that matters.
- **2 kg dumbbells.** Below the load threshold where anything happens. Not "light" — absent.

### The gaps in a typical self-built routine

A squat + push-up circuit is anterior-chain and chest. Missing, almost always:

```
hinge (RDL / single-leg RDL)         glutes + hamstrings
hamstring eccentric (Nordic curl)    the one true eccentric gap
calf / Achilles (slow heel drop)     highest-incidence runner injury
slow lowering (3-4 s) on everything
```

**Eccentric is the real gap and the cheapest to close.** Running contains almost no eccentric
overload (a sprint is concentric/elastic), and late-race form collapse — stride shortening, vertical
ratio rising, GCT lengthening — is usually eccentric control going first. **But the anchor that matters
is the ankles, not the door** — a Nordic needs the feet *fixed* (hook them under the bottom stair
tread, a loaded sofa leg, or a closed door with a towel under it). No anchor available? Three
substitutes keep most of the eccentric:

```
single-leg RDL, loaded, 4 s lowering        the best available hip-hinge eccentric
slow single-leg step-down, 3-4 s            quad eccentric, mimics landing
band leg-curl with a slow return            hamstring, concentric band / eccentric control
```

That is roughly **70-80 %** of a Nordic's effect — say the number, so the athlete knows it is a
substitution and not a downgrade to nothing.

### Plyometrics: horizontal beats vertical for a distance runner — and needs no box

"I don't have a 30-40 cm box" is not a constraint, it is a reason to pick the better exercise. A depth
drop jump trains **vertical** power; running is **horizontal** propulsion. Bounding, single-leg hops and
standing long jumps are therefore both **more specific** and **equipment-free**.

So prescribe horizontal plyo as the default, not as a substitute: 3x4/leg bounding, 3x4/leg single-leg
hops, 3x3 standing long jumps ~= 24 ground contacts. Surface matters (grass, dirt, track — never
concrete); quality matters more than count, which is why 40 tired jumps in a circuit is conditioning,
not power.

## Is external load necessary?

Not for the adaptation that matters. **It becomes necessary when the exercise stops being
high-intensity:**

```
25+ clean single-leg squats   ->  no longer a strength stimulus, now local endurance
```

Four load sources, and a barbell is only one:

| source | how |
|---|---|
| unilateral | load doubles; also more specific than running (which is single-leg support) |
| leverage | rear foot elevated on a split squat |
| eccentric | 3-4 s lowering, slow step-downs, Nordic |
| velocity | jumps — high force at bodyweight |

**No-gym substitution that actually works**: single-leg + a 20-30 kg loaded backpack is roughly a
barbell double-leg lift at 60 kg. That is already far past "bodyweight".

Also worth stating: a 5K/10K runner is already doing **2.5-3x bodyweight** vertical ground reaction
force at race pace. Force production is not the missing piece — **eccentric control and tendon
stiffness** are.

## Placement — the rules that actually bind

1. **Lower-body strength at least 48 h from a hard session**, and **never the morning after the
   hardest one** (see the cost-ranking section in `endurance-training-analysis`).
2. **Plyometrics first in the session.** Jump quality is neural and dies under fatigue; a session that
   ends with 40 tired squat jumps trains tolerance, not power.
3. **Doubles go only on days whose *evening* session is easy.** A morning run before an evening
   quality session buys volume and spends the week's most expensive asset.
4. **Trunk work is the one quality that is MORE specific when fatigued** — running's trunk demand is
   anti-rotation/anti-extension *under load*, so make it a **finisher on the easy runs** (last 12 min
   of 2-3 easy days), not a session and not a morning. It needs no freshness (unlike plyo and heavy
   load), it costs no scheduling slot, and it releases every morning it used to occupy. Those mornings
   are the natural home of future double-run days — freeing them now is the point.
5. **In the highest-volume weeks, one strength session beats two.** The volume itself is the stimulus;
   DOMS is what eats it.

## Core work is not a strength session

It fails every condition that makes a strength session one:

| condition | met? |
|---|---|
| high external load (>=80 % 1RM) | no |
| high-velocity output (jumps, sprints) | no |
| meaningful systemic stress | no |

So it cannot drive the neural adaptation or the tendon stiffness that economy comes from. Its real job
is different: **not becoming the weak link when the trunk starts collapsing late in a race.**

Because the cost is near zero it runs on a separate budget — 10-12 min, 2-3x/week, **never to
failure** (failure-cost on trunk work leaks into running posture).

### Retire the core programme with a two-number self-test

Before arguing about exercise selection, ask whether trunk endurance is a limiter **at all**. Two holds
settle it in three minutes:

| test | adequate | reading |
|---|---|---|
| side plank | **>= 90 s/side** | trunk endurance is not the limiter -> core drops to **maintenance**, and the 50 min/week it was eating belongs to running |
| plank | **>= 2 min** (McGill norm ~90-120 s) | same |
| side plank **< 45 s/side** | — | real gap: 6 weeks of 3x45 s *before* any reduction is discussed |

Worked case: athlete reported 90 s+ / 2 min+ and had 19 months injury-free, a fastest-last-rep 6x1000
and a hard session where HR hit the ceiling before the legs did — so the trunk had never been the first
thing to fail. **A capacity that is already adequate is not developed by adding volume; it is
maintained, and the surplus is spent elsewhere.**

**The more discriminating number is left-vs-right, not the absolute hold.** <10 % asymmetry is noise;
>20 % is a real finding about single-leg pelvic control and is worth chasing even when both sides pass
the absolute threshold.

A worked reduction of a 60-set, 30-minute routine down to four movements:

```
anti-rotation    band Pallof press          3x10/side
anti-extension   plank, feet elevated       3x30 s
lateral          side plank                 3x30 s/side
hip extension    single-leg bridge, loaded  3x8/leg
```

## The verification metric

Do not verify strength by adding weight. Verify it by **ground-contact time on easy runs trending
down at matched pace.** If load rose and GCT did not move, the strength work is not converting into
economy — change the exercise, not the dose.
