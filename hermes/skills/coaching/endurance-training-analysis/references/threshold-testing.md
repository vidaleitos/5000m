# Locating threshold without a lactate meter

Companion to the "Measurement design" section. Everything here is for an athlete with **no lactate
analyser and no lab** — which is the normal situation, so treat it as the default rather than a
fallback.

---

## 1. Why a 30-minute test, and what it actually returns

A 30-min maximal effort sits close enough to maximal-lactate-steady-state that it gives two usable
outputs at once. It is the standard meter-free substitute (error ~1–2 % on the HR side).

| Output of the test | What it is |
|---|---|
| **average HR over the 30:00** | **LTHR** — the more reliable of the two |
| **average pace** | **not** LT2 pace (see the conversion below) |
| HR at min 5 / 10 / 15 / 20 / 25 / 30 | whether the effort sat at or above MLSS |

### The conversion that gets skipped

LT2 is defined as the intensity sustainable for roughly **60 minutes**. A 30-min maximal effort is
faster than that, so:

```
LT2 pace  ≈  30-min average pace  +  5 – 10 s/km

worked: 30-min test = 4:05/km  ->  LT2 ≈ 4:10 – 4:15/km
```

Use it raw "because that's what the test says" and every threshold prescription lands about one pace
band too fast. This is the single most common error with this test.

---

## 2. Protocol

**"All out" means "as even as possible", not "sprint".** Over a fixed 30:00 every positive split leaves
distance on the road; the maximum-distance run is the one that is nearly level from 5 min to 30 min. It
is a pacing exam, not a willpower exam.

```
warm-up     15 min + 2-3 x 30 s strides

0-5 min     NO faster than <ceiling>        # pacing guard, e.g. 4:15/km
5-25 min    hold the fastest pace that stays repeatable
25-30 min   push only if something is left; if sinking, the opening was too fast

stop at exactly 30:00
record      total distance, avgHR, HR at 5/10/15/20/25/30 min
```

**The 0–5 min ceiling is not optional** with an athlete who has previously blown a time trial. It costs a
few metres of distance and buys a valid reading.

**Schedule it on clean legs.** Putting it where the threshold session normally sits is ideal, but the
preceding quality day has to be moved or dropped — a 30-min TT 48 h after a rep session reads as a
fitness decline that is not there. A test week doubles as a deload week, which is the cheapest way to
place it.

---

## 3. Reading the result

| Step | Gives |
|---|---|
| avgHR over the 30 min | **LTHR** |
| avg pace + 5–10 s/km | **LT2 pace** |
| HR drift, min 10 → min 30 | `≤ 3 bpm` = at/below MLSS · `> 5 bpm` with pace flat = **above** MLSS |

If **pace falls** while HR holds or rises, the opening was too fast and the test under-reads — say so
rather than banking the number.

---

## 4. The other four meter-free checks

Use these between tests; they also keep a bad test from being trusted.

**a. Heart-rate drift during the session** — the most useful one, because it reports *while you run*.
A plateau after ~10 min means at-or-below threshold; steady upward drift at constant pace means above.

**b. Last rep must not slow.** In a rep set, holding pace to the final rep with controlled RPE = at or
below threshold. See the direction table in the main skill — a fast final rep means the opposite.

**c. Breathing.** Near LT2, speech runs to roughly **3–4 words per breath**. Whole sentences = below;
cannot speak = above.

**d. Longitudinal: pace at fixed HR.** Two same-banded easy runs weeks apart. Needs no extra effort and
measures *change* rather than an absolute — which is often the question that actually matters.

---

## 5. Which way to err, when you do not know

The costs are **asymmetric**, and that decides the direction:

| Error | You pay | You get |
|---|---|---|
| slightly **below** LT2 | a little less adaptation per minute | still full aerobic volume, low fatigue, next session intact |
| slightly **above** LT2 | fatigue rises steeply | the extra is mostly anaerobic cost; aerobic adaptation barely increases, and the next session is polluted |

> **Bias low when uncertain.** This is arithmetic, not caution.

**But the rule only applies while you do not know.** Once there is evidence the athlete is *under* the
band — e.g. threshold reps reported as "felt easy" — the move is to raise the pace, not to keep biasing
down.

---

## 6. Where the "wasted" volume actually comes from

Three sources, with different fixes:

| Source | Tell | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| **too slow** — recovery pace counted as aerobic | high share of easy running at HR ≤ 130 | floor: designated aerobic runs at HR ≥ 140 |
| **too fast** — above threshold in threshold work | last rep slows; HR drifts up at constant pace | ceiling: the two criteria above |
| **no target at all** — sessions with no number | quality volume drifting between bands | one pace number per session |

The first is usually the largest by volume, and the least visible — it never shows up in a quality-session
review.
