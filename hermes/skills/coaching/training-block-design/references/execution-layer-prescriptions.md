# Execution-layer prescriptions — worked criteria

These are the answers that stopped follow-up questions. Each one is **purpose + dose + the check that
says it was enough** — never a slogan. The athlete asked about every item below in one session;
treat them as a template for how to answer the next execution question.

---

## Warm-up

**Three purposes, and "sweating" is not one of them:**

1. raise muscle and core temperature → faster nerve conduction, lower muscle viscosity
2. ramp heart rate and ventilation gradually → avoid a cardiovascular shock at the start of the main set
3. neural activation and movement preparation — **not** stretching

Structure: progressive jog → dynamic drills → strides → short rest → main set.

| Main set | Warm-up |
|---|---|
| Easy run / recovery | **none needed** — the first 1–2 km *is* the warm-up |
| Threshold | jog 2.5–3 km + dynamic drills + 4 × 60–80 m strides |
| Race-pace / special block | jog 3 km + dynamic drills + 4–6 × 60–80 m strides + 2–3 min rest |
| Hill / stair sprints | jog 1.5–2 km + dynamic drills + 3–4 × 60 m strides |
| Time trial / race | jog 3 km + dynamic drills + 4–6 × 60–80 m strides + 3–4 min rest |

### When the session *is* the warm-up

Very common (e.g. "9 km easy → strides → stair sprints"). **Do not add a separate warm-up jog.**

```
9 km easy (first 1 km 20–30 s/km slower, easing in)
  ↓ already hot
5 min dynamic drills  (5, not 8–10 — heating is done; this is range-of-motion + neural only)
  ↓
dynamic strides / accelerations (the specific activation)
  ↓ 2–3 min
main set
  ↓
cool-down: walk or jog 1–2 km
```

### Criteria

1. 8–10 reps (or 20 m) per drill, **one round only** — not three
2. **Not fatiguing**: breathing supports normal speech; HR not clearly above the warm-up jog
3. Move to *today's comfortable end range*, not to pain. This is waking up, not flexibility work
4. 5–8 min total; **8–10 min in the early morning** (lowest body temperature, slowest neural activation)

**Check: the first stride should feel "the legs are alive".** If it still feels stiff, the dynamic
work was insufficient or lacked a raising component — drills 6–8 below do that job.

**Static stretching goes after the run, never before one.** 20–30 s holds transiently reduce force
output; before a quality or strength session that is a negative.

### Dynamic drill set (8, one round)

| # | Drill | Dose | Purpose |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | ankle circles + heel raises | 10/side | ankle range + calf wake-up |
| 2 | hip circles (single leg, hand on wall) | 8/side | hip range |
| 3 | front/back leg swings | 10/side | hip flexion/extension range (no bouncing) |
| 4 | lateral leg swings | 10/side | hip adduction/abduction |
| 5 | lunge with rotation | 6/side | hip flexor + thoracic |
| 6 | high-knee walk | 20 m | hip flexor activation |
| 7 | butt kicks | 20 m | hamstring |
| 8 | A-skip | 20 m | coordination + forefoot contact pattern |

**Minimum viable set when time is short: 1 + 3 + 6 + 8** (ankle, leg swings, high-knee walk, A-skip) —
under two minutes.

## Strides / accelerations (100 m)

**Not a sprint, not maximal.** ~90–95 % "controlled fast"; the feeling is *light, relaxed, could go
faster*.

Anchor with the athlete's own data when available. In the case studied, accelerations were already
landing at 2:32–2:55 /km peak with a ground-contact time of 156–182 ms — versus 197 ms at race pace and
~242 ms at easy pace. **That gap is the whole reason the drill exists**: it proves the system can reach a
much shorter contact time than race pace currently uses.

### Three-phase structure

1. **0–30 m progressive acceleration** — never a sudden start; shift up through the gears
2. **30–70 m hold** — the important part. *Relax.* Torso upright/very slightly forward, shoulders and
   hands loose, arms swinging front-to-back, not across
3. **70–100 m float out** — **never brake.** Let speed decay on its own

### Technique cues that fit a stride-length-dominant runner

- **Contact light, short, quiet.** Footfall noise is the feedback channel — loud = slamming
- **Do not reach for a longer stride.** Speed comes from ground reaction and hip drive, not from
  extending the leg
- Cue: *"push the ground backwards", not "pull the body forwards"*
- Foot lands **under the body**, not out in front

### Set rules

- First 2: slower, as activation. Middle 4: best quality. Last 2: hold quality
- **If reps 7–8 degrade, stop at 6.** The rep count is not the goal
- Recovery: walk or jog back, **60–90 s**
- Surface: flat, clean, hard (asphalt / track). Not grass, not uneven paving
- The stride metres and the recovery jog are **not counted** inside the prescribed easy distance

## Cool-down

**Optional. Not mandatory. Form is free.** It contributes almost nothing to adaptation — it does not
reduce next-day soreness, does not improve next-day performance, does not improve training effect.

Where it genuinely matters, only two cases:
1. **After an all-out effort** (time trial, race) — prevents blood pooling and dizziness. Walk 10 min.
2. **After a very hard session in the heat** — lets core temperature come down gradually.

After a hill/stair session: walking home is sufficient.

**Form:** walk 5–10 min / jog 1 km / nothing / static stretching if wanted — all acceptable.

**One principle: a cool-down lowers intensity, it does not add volume.** Jogging an extra 2 km after a
quality session is not a cool-down, it is adding load while fatigued — a negative.

**Recording:** do not record it separately. Walking home is not training. If a jogging cool-down is
recorded at all, lap it off — a slow low-HR tail inside the activity drags the session average down and
contaminates any steady-session efficiency analysis.

## Where strength and hill/stair work sit in the week

The governing rule is not "hours since the last session" (see SKILL.md § 3) — it is **distance from the
next quality session**, read in the warm-up.

A workable default shape, with the quality sessions as the fixed points:

| Day | Run | Strength / neuromuscular |
|---|---|---|
| Mon | medium run | — |
| Tue | easy | mini-band activation + plyometric day |
| Wed | **threshold (quality)** | **nothing** |
| Thu | easy + strides | **stair sprints → then single-leg strength** |
| Fri | easy | — |
| Sat | **race pace (quality)** | **nothing** |
| Sun | long run (pure aerobic) | — |

Two hard constraints if this shape is used:
1. **Strength total 20–25 min, never to failure.** The moment it is trained "to soreness" it starts
   stealing the quality sessions, which is where all the value is.
2. **If the quality session degrades, drop one strength slot — keep the one furthest from quality.**

**Order within a day:** hill/stair sprints first (high force output while the nervous system is fresh),
then the low-velocity strength work. Both after the run.

**If the long run follows a quality session by only ~24 h, it must be pure aerobic.** No fast finish,
no progression. This was wrong in an early version of a plan — the fast finish had to be removed
because the week's spacing made it a third hard day.

### Hill vs stairs — what changes

Stairs are the better default: bigger vertical component, shorter contact time, far easier to find. The
main practical failure is **segment length** — if total vertical gain ÷ reps is small (~5 m per climb),
the athlete tops out in a few seconds and never reaches a stable high-force phase; the stimulus is about
half a real 10–12 s hill rep. Fix by finding a longer continuous climb, **not** by adding reps.

## What to ask the athlete to record

**Near zero.** Aim for the following and nothing more:

- Short-effort sessions (stairs/hills): **rep times** (Garmin auto-lap is enough), plus one sentence —
  *"did the last rep still feel like fast ground contact, yes/no"*, plus a 1–10 effort rating.
- Everything else: record the session normally, exactly as they always have.

**Do not make the athlete pre-segment their own data.** Filtering, deduplication and steady-session
selection are analysis-time operations. Say so explicitly — *"train, and I'll handle the data"* —
because a new recording chore is a new way to add friction to the one thing that has to stay easy.

For short efforts, HR is worse than useless: an 11 s rep physically cannot express intensity in the HR
trace, and wrist optical HR degrades badly under the arm swing and wrist flexion of stair running. If
the athlete reports the session was hard, **believe the athlete and retract any judgement made from the
HR trace.**
