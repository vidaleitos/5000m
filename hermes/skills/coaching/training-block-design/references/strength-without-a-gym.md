# Strength work with no gym, no hills, no partner

Constraints that were stated once and must not be re-litigated: no gym, bodyweight plus a pair of
2 kg dumbbells, no usable hill, no training partner, no track. Available: **stairs**, a small loop
band (mini band only — no long anchored band), a backpack, one day with an option of two sessions.

---

## 1. Check what the equipment was actually for, before designing a fallback

The instinct is to rank tools and then hand over a degraded version. Do the opposite: ask what
**stimulus** each item was providing, then ask whether the equipment was necessary for it.

| Equipment item | What it actually provided | Bodyweight equivalent | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| Mini band — lateral walk | glute med under single-leg load | **side-lying leg raise** (pelvis stays stacked) | substitute is **better isolated** |
| Mini band — clamshell | glute med, hip external rotation | **single-leg glute bridge** | substitute is **better isolated** |
| Mini band — ankle dorsiflexion | tibialis anterior (matters directly for fast ground contact) | **toe-walk / wall tibialis contraction** | substitute is **equivalent** |
| Jump rope | elastic plyometric + heart rate | **pogo hops** — already in the programme | **duplicate, delete** |
| Backpack (5→15 kg) | progressive overload for single-leg work | — | genuinely useful, but not needed until the loading phase |
| Stairs (already available) | high force + short contact + single-leg | — | **the best tool the athlete owns** |

**Conclusion: the equipment-free version is not a downgrade.** Two of the highest-value items (pogo
hops, single-leg strength) need nothing. So the answer to *"what should I do with no equipment?"*
is **"you don't need it"** — not a hierarchy of progressively worse options.

Corollary: never let a purchase list you suggested harden into a requirement you then design around.

---

## 2. The only two stimuli that matter here

1. **Fast ground contact** — tendon stiffness and the ability to leave the ground quickly. This is what
   moves ground-contact time, and ground-contact time at race pace is the mechanical limiter.
2. **Force production on one leg** — because running is a series of single-leg hops, and speed in this
   athlete comes from stride length (corr(speed, step length) ≈ 0.99) rather than cadence.

Everything else is accessory. Glute med and tibialis anterior are worth having but have **no known gap**
in an athlete with 19 months of zero injury — rank them below the two above, and skip them without
ceremony when time is short.

---

## 3. The zero-equipment set

- **Pogo hops** — the most direct short-contact drill that exists. `2–4 × 15`.
- **Single-leg calf raise, full range, off a step edge** — the Achilles/calf spring *is* ground-contact
  time. `2–3 × 12/leg`.
- **Bulgarian split squat, rear foot elevated, bodyweight** — the front leg carries roughly 85 % of body
  mass through a full range; this is the ceiling for unloaded single-leg force. `2–3 × 6–8/leg`.
- **Single-leg RDL** — posterior chain plus balance. `2–3 × 8/leg`.
- **Wall sit, single leg** — isometric tendon load. `2 × 30–60 s`.

Anchor it to **stair sprints**, which deliver both stimuli at once and need no equipment.

### Technique cues (these define whether the session worked)

**Pogo hops** — feet hip-width, **knees barely involved**, trunk upright. Ankles only; think of the sole
as a spring with the knee angle unchanged. Contact is **extremely short** — almost a touch-and-go.
Sound should be a light *pat*, never a *thud*. Height 3–5 cm, not a jump for height. The sensation
belongs in the **Achilles and lower calf**. The common error is bending the knees, which makes it a mini
squat-jump and trains quadriceps instead of elasticity.

**Single-leg calf raise** — forefoot on a step edge, heel hanging, hand on a wall. **Lower slowly
(3 s)**, feeling the Achilles lengthen, then **rise fast** and hold 1 s at full extension. Full range
plus a slow eccentric is the whole point; halving the range to add reps means the session did nothing.

**Bulgarian split squat** — rear foot on a step or bed edge, front foot about a step and a half forward.
Trunk **leaning slightly forward (~15°)** so load sits on the mid-forefoot. Descend vertically, front
knee tracking over the toes, to about thigh-parallel — no deeper. Drive up through the front foot; the
rear leg does not help. Feel it in the **front-leg glute and quadriceps**. Errors: front foot too close
(knee dives), rear leg pushing, trunk bolt upright.

**Single-leg RDL** — hips travel back, trunk tips toward horizontal, standing knee soft. Stretch should
appear in the **standing leg's posterior chain**.

## 4. The quality rule (non-negotiable half of the session)

Volume is flexible; quality is not.

- **Do not jump cold.** Warm up first — the mini band's real job, if you have one, is to *be* the warm-up.
- **Do not jump when the nervous system is fatigued** — never immediately after a quality session.
  Sloppy jumps are both ineffective and the actual injury risk.
- **Stop when the movement deforms.** Two clean sets beat four ugly ones.

Dose: **2–3 sets, 20–25 minutes total, never to failure**, and **you should not be out of breath when
you finish** — this trains the nervous system, not the heart. Rest 60–90 s between sets.

---

## 5. Where it sits in the week — doubles are the *best* slot, not an obstacle

This is counterintuitive and it matters. The athlete assumed a two-session day made strength harder to
place. It makes it easier:

- **The second session of a double is necessarily easy** (rule: never two quality sessions in one day).
- **That easy run is the warm-up.** The athlete finishes it warm, with heart rate still up, already
  standing outside.
- **It costs no extra slot** — no separate trip, no change of clothes, no extra warm-up.

Hence the placement rule: **on a double day, put strength after the second (easy) run.** If the day is
*quality AM + easy PM*, this is even clearer — **never after the AM quality session** (CNS fatigue),
**always after the PM easy run**.

The genuinely awkward case is the **single easy run followed by having to leave immediately.** That is
the only shape that needs an independent slot — and even then, a 5-minute version beats nothing.

The design principle: **strength's real obstacle is never equipment or time — it is that it has no
reasoned slot.** Give it a slot with a *structural* justification ("that day, that moment, you are
necessarily warm, not fatigued, and already there") rather than a clock-based one.

## 6. Retracted positions (do not let these come back)

| Item | Verdict | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| **Nordic hamstring curl** | deleted | no ankle fixation available; its evidence base is *injury prevention*, and the athlete had 19 months of zero injury — the gap did not exist |
| **Banded resisted sprint** | deleted | only a short mini band obtainable; insufficient resistance and length |
| **Jump rope** | deleted | duplicates pogo hops |
| **"Do the plyometrics in a 10-minute window right after the run"** | retracted | assumed the athlete could carry a band and rope to the run location. They cannot. Fixed by moving the whole session indoors, accepting a 3-minute self-warm-up — **a 3-minute cost beats squeezing the session into a window that does not exist** |
| **"Run warm-up and cool-down are optional so exclude them"** | wrong → two-number rule | they are real physiological load and the historical data was counted the same way; they belong in *total* volume and not in *stimulus* volume (see `endurance-training-analysis`) |
