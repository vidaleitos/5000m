# Is the substitute equivalent? — the eight-dimension check

"Same muscle group" is not a substitution test. Two movements can hit the same muscle and differ in
five other ways that all matter. Worked out when the athlete asked directly:
*"你寻找的替代动作是否完全等效替代了所有收益，如果没有可以用多个动作来弥补收益"* — and answered
it himself in the same breath: **"替代不一定是一对一的关系"** (a substitution need not be one-to-one).

---

## 1. The eight dimensions

Check every one. A substitute is equivalent only when all eight match.

| # | Dimension | Why it changes the adaptation |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Target muscles** | the obvious one — and the least informative on its own |
| 2 | **Force direction** | gravity-down vs horizontal vs transverse-rotational load different structures |
| 3 | **Contraction type** | isometric / concentric / eccentric / elastic |
| 4 | **Body position** | lying / kneeling / standing / unilateral — this sets the *stability* demand, and therefore whether the trunk and scapula get trained as a side effect |
| 5 | **Progressibility** | does it have a load or lever path, or does it dead-end? |
| 6 | **Joint angle / ROM** | the same muscle at a different length is a different stimulus |
| 7 | **Co-demand** | does the original require something *else* to work at the same time (e.g. trunk holding while the arms move)? |
| 8 | **Fatigue pattern** | local vs whole-body, and how long it takes to recover |

> **A substitution is a *set*, not a swap.** The right question is *"does the set cover the dimensions?"*
> — not *"is this the same exercise?"*

Missing dimensions get filled with **additional movements**. Say how many of the eight are covered.
Never imply full equivalence when it is not there, and never present a multi-movement set as though the
athlete is getting the original back.

## 2. Worked substitutions (one athlete's actual kit)

### 2a. Pallof press → dumbbell anti-rotation hold + single-arm plank (+ two more)

| Dim | Pallof | DB front-raise hold | Single-arm plank | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 target | ✓ | ✓ | ✓ | full |
| 2 **force direction** | **horizontal rotation** | vertical (creates a rotation moment) | gravity support | **missing** |
| 3 contraction | iso + conc + ecc | mostly isometric | isometric | **missing dynamic / eccentric** |
| 4 position | standing / half-kneel / split | standing | lying | complementary |
| 5 progressibility | strong (grade, distance, single leg) | medium | medium | partial |

Fill the two gaps with two more movements:

- **Self-resistance rotation** — 10–15 s × 3/side. Hands clasped at the chest; one hand tries to rotate
  the trunk, the other resists. **The body does not actually turn — it is a contest, not a movement.**
  Fills: **dynamic + rotation-direction force.**
- **Wall-push anti-rotation** — 15–20 s/side. Stand side-on, one hand pushing the wall, resist the
  turning tendency. Fills: **the horizontal force direction.**

Still missing after all four: **progressively loadable external resistance.** Say so plainly — it needs
a long band or a cable.

### 2b. Band external rotation → dumbbell side-lying external rotation

| Dim | Band | DB side-lying | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 target | ✓ | ✓ (isolates better) | full |
| 3 contraction | tension + elastic | tension + better eccentric control | full |
| 4 **position** | standing / seated | side-lying (supported) | **missing standing** |
| 7 **co-demand** | standing demands scapular stability | side-lying supports the scapula | **missing** |

Fill: **standing isometric external rotation against a wall** — 10 s × 3 angles (forearm slightly
forward / directly sideways / slightly back). Standing position and the scapular-stability demand are
now back. Elbow stays pinned to the body throughout; this trains the cuff, not the arm.

### 2c. Band pull-apart → prone Y-T-W

| Dim | Pull-apart | Y-T-W | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 target | ✓ | ✓ (isolates better) | full |
| 2 force direction | horizontal | anti-gravity | **missing horizontal** |
| 4 **position** | standing | prone | **missing standing** |
| 5 progressibility | capped by band length | strong (isometric hold, dumbbells) | full |

Fill: **standing scapular retraction isometric**, 20–30 s.
Bonus use: while holding it, feel the sides of the neck — if upper trapezius fires first or feels
harder than mid/lower trap, that is the **shrugging-compensation test coming back positive**, and it is
the cheapest of the outstanding diagnostic tests to run.

### The pattern across all three

In every case the missing dimension was **body position / co-demand**, and the fix was a cheap
**standing isometric**. The target muscles were never the problem. Check dimensions 4 and 7 early — they
are the ones that quietly disappear when a movement changes from standing to lying.

## 3. Two schemes is the right answer shape

The athlete asked: *"如果当某些动作在没有哑铃、凳子和悬挂杆的时候 … 应该如何等效替代弥补。
算上当前方案给两个方案"* — two schemes, counting the current one.

What worked, in this order:

1. **A per-item A/B table over the whole programme**, third column for the stimulus delta
   (`same` / `close` / `degrades` / `absent`).
2. **Then the headline count**, so the real size of the problem is visible instead of the athlete
   assuming everything degrades. Here: **13 items, 10 unchanged or close, 3 genuinely affected.**
3. **Then detail only on the items that change.**
4. **Then the irreducible gaps, in a short table.**
5. **Then what to restore first** when equipment comes back.

Done in the other order (gaps first), a minor equipment change reads as a programme collapse.

---

## 4. The fully-bodyweight version, and its two real gaps

Conditions: no dumbbell, no bench or step, no bar, no table, no band — **floor, wall and bodyweight
only.**

| # | With equipment | Bodyweight only | Delta |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | towel-roll thoracic extension | **quadruped, sink the chest toward the floor**; or upper back over a wall corner | close |
| 2 | door-frame pec stretch | same (a wall is not equipment) | same |
| 3 | kneeling lat stretch on a table edge | **quadruped, hands on the floor, hips back, chest down** | same — arguably better controlled |
| 4 | wall angel | same | same |
| 5 | standing arm swing | same | same |
| 6 | **feet-supported partial hang** | **nothing equivalent**; nearest is a plank-position scapular depression + protraction | **absent** |
| 7 | **inverted row** | prone straight-arm extension **+** prone T/Y/W isometrics **+** towel self-resistance row — all three needed | **degrades sharply** |
| 8 | pull-apart / Y-T-W | prone T raise + Y-T-W | close |
| 8b | standing scapular retraction isometric | same | same |
| 9 | DB side-lying external rotation | standing isometric ER against a wall + self-resistance ER + prone T thumbs-up | degrades |
| 10 | scapular push-up | same | same |
| 11–13 | bird dog / side plank / dead bug | same (drop the loaded progressions) | same |
| 14a | DB single-arm anti-rotation hold | **single-leg stand + arm forward** | degrades |
| 14b–d | single-arm plank / self-resistance / wall push | same | same |

### The irreducible gaps — there are only two

| Gap | Why | Consequence |
|---|---|---|
| **Traction tolerance** | only hanging trains it; plank-position scapular depression loads the *opposite* direction (compression, not traction) | overhead / traction capacity **stalls** |
| **Pulling strength** | the prone series plus towel self-resistance cannot approach rowing loads | **maintained, not progressed** |

Rotator cuff is *not* a true gap — isometrics plus self-resistance maintain it adequately.

**So the bodyweight version is a transit / maintenance programme, not an equivalent.** Say it in those
words. Then name what to restore first — and note that neither item needs a purchase, only environment:

- **Inverted row** — a table, a door, a rail, a tree branch; any one of them.
- **Feet-supported partial hang** — a bar and something to stand on.

Those two are the only things the bodyweight version cannot reach, and they are also the two most
valuable items in the whole upper-body programme. Restore them first.
