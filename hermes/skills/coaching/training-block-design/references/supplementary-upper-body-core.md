# Prescribing upper-body and core work for a runner

Supplementary work that runs *alongside* the main programme, for an athlete whose report was:

> 长距离或者强度课比较长时，肩膀会很酸、变沉。上肢比较薄弱，后背很弱，上肢除了腹部有一点力量以外都比较薄弱。

---

## 1. No movement reaches the floor without a stated reason

Supplementary work is where programmes bloat. Every item must be traceable to either the athlete's own
reported problem or their limiting factor. This is why the *reported* weak link outranks the
*theoretically* weak link:

- **Upper back** — the athlete reported it → highest priority.
- **Core** — the athlete did **not** report it, and matched-pace data showed **no late-session
  mechanical decay** (ground-contact time identical in km 5 and km 14 of a long run; *shorter* in rep 3
  than rep 1 of a threshold session). Late trunk collapse is where a core gap surfaces. It did not
  surface. So core is allowed, but **ranked below the upper back** — if a week has three 5-minute gaps,
  all three go to the upper back.

### Why shoulder work is not peripheral for a stride-length runner

Arm swing is half the drive of the stride. For an athlete whose speed comes almost entirely from step
length (corr(speed, step length) ≈ 0.99), a failing shoulder girdle is a failing drivetrain:

```
scapula loses position → arm-swing amplitude drops → trunk rotation leaks → step length drops
scapula anterior tilt → limits thoracic extension → limits breathing amplitude
```

The second line links the same weakness to the athlete's actual limiter (aerobic). So this is
not cosmetic. **Confidence: medium** — arm swing's contribution to step length is real but its
magnitude is contested in the literature. The athlete's own subjective report (where their fatigue
arrives first) is the harder evidence, and it should outrank the papers.

### The classification test

Does the effect depend on **when** it is done? If not, it is not scheduled — it is hooked. See the
schedule-vs-hook subsection in `training-block-design` §7. Upper back and core are hook category; the
only hard rule is **never to failure**.

---

## 2. The capability chain — the correction that matters most

An earlier version of this programme was framed as *"the goal is not strength, it is position
retention"*. The athlete identified the hole:

> **The goal is not hypertrophy. Strength, function and coordination are all goals. Circumference is
> only a result.**

If the goal is stated as *not strength*, progressive overload becomes optional — and progressive
overload is the root driver of adaptation. **The programme then plateaus and the cause is invisible.**
The concrete error: *strength is a continuum.* Failing at minute 60 of a long run is a
**strength-endurance** problem — a branch of strength, not "not strength".

> **coordination/position → strength → strength-endurance → event-specific**

- **Stage 1 — coordination/position.** Position only: no load, no extended duration. Load destroys a
  pattern that is still being built. This is the only stage with that character.
- **Stage 2 — strength.** **As soon as position is stable, add load.** Zero-load duration extension
  degenerates into *holding with very little force for a long time* — that trains nothing.
- **Stage 3 — strength-endurance.** Add duration **under load**. This is the direct training for the
  reported symptom.
- **Stage 4 — event-specific.** Used under fatigue (the last 10 minutes of a quality session, the
  final 10 km of a long run). Only after stage 3 — practising an unstable position when tired is
  practising compensation.

Load and duration then advance **in parallel**. One variable at a time. Escalating load means
**difficulty angle**, not weight: lower the feet on a row, lengthen the moment arm, add a pause.

---

## 3. Upper body

### Stage 1 — position (zero load)

| Movement | Dose | What it is for |
|---|---|---|
| **Wall angel** | 2 × 10 | The single most on-target item. Back, glutes, head against the wall, arms and the backs of the hands also on the wall, slide from W to Y and back. It is the *inverse* of scapular anterior tilt and restores thoracic extension — on which both arm swing and breathing depend. |
| **Prone Y-T-W** | 2 × 8/direction | Mid/lower trapezius, rhomboids, posterior deltoid. **Press the shoulders down and back before lifting** — reversed order recruits upper trap and neck instead. |
| **Standing W isometric** | 5 × 10 s | Holds the scapula in retraction/depression. Ribs down, pelvis neutral. |
| **Unloaded fast arm swing** | 2 × 45 s | Highest specificity — the actual running action, at endurance duration. Elbows stay in the vertical plane at the sides; forward/back only, no lateral swaying. |

**These are a chain, not four exercises:** the first two put the scapula back in position, the hold
keeps it there, the arm swing uses it while holding. **Order matters.**

### Stage 2 — strength (add load)

- **Inverted row, 3 sets** — under a table / park bar / door handle with a towel. **The only source of
  an upper limit on upper-body strength.** Add it the moment position is stable; do not wait.
- **Scapular push-up 2 × 8** or **push-up-plus 2 × 8–12** — serratus anterior (scapular upward rotation
  and protraction). Pick one. **The reason is the serratus, not the chest.** Plain push-ups are not
  prescribed: no functional value for running, and extra chest volume worsens the anterior tilt.
- **Lying straight-arm pullover, 15–20 × 2–3** — the lats, with the long-arm lever (see the long-lever
  note in `training-block-design` §7).
- **Prone Y-T-W with 2 kg dumbbells.**

### Stage 3 — strength-endurance (duration under load)

W isometric 10 → 20 → 30 s without the retraction force decaying · Y-T-W held 20–30 s · row with a slow
eccentric and a 2 s pause at the top, or 15–20 reps · arm swing to 90 s, then with dumbbells.

#### The longest set must eventually reach the duration of the demand

The athlete's correction, and it exposed a whole missing axis:

> *"以后可能需要加入：肩胛下沉控制、前锯肌耐力、**低负荷长时间保持** —— 因为跑步摆臂不是最大力量问题，*
> *而是 90 分钟以内保持正确肩胛位置的能力。"*

**The first version of this programme's longest set was 30 seconds, against a 90-minute demand.** That
is a **dose mismatch**, and it is invisible if you think only along a load axis. This athlete's failure
point is 65–70 minutes; nothing in a 20–30 s set trains a 70-minute hold.

So the module needs **three axes, not two**:

| Axis | Trains | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **Load** | makes the low load a smaller fraction | row, partial hang, scapular push-up |
| **Duration** | raises the ceiling at low load | **long holds — 60–120 s** |
| **Control** | accuracy of the position itself | wall angel, scapular depression, serratus |

Long-hold items, added specifically for this:

- **Standing scapular retraction + depression, held 60–120 s** — not for force, for *the position not
  decaying*. The closest training match to the actual demand.
- **Prone Y hold, 45–60 s** — lift to position and stay; no shrug anywhere in it.
- **Wall angel, isometric version** — hold at a point on the slide for 30–45 s.

**Check before writing any accessory dose:** is the longest set within an order of magnitude of the
duration over which the quality must survive? A 90-minute demand trained with sub-minute sets is a
different quality being trained.

### Stage 4 — under fatigue

2 × 60 s fast arm swing **immediately after a quality session** — the scapula is already at the
position where it will fail in a race. Plus a deliberate intent to hold elbow position through the
last 10 minutes of a long run.

## 4. Core

**The demand is *anti*: anti-rotation, anti-lateral-flexion, anti-extension.** Not flexion.

- **Bird dog 2 × 6/side, 3 s hold** — anti-rotation. A glass of water on the back; the extending leg
  does not go above hip height.
- **Side plank 2 × 20–30 s/side** — anti-lateral-flexion. Hip lifted, one straight line; knee version
  is a legitimate start. Running is single-leg loading, which is where this earns its place.
- **Dead bug 2 × 8/side** — anti-extension. A hand under the lower back; the moment it lifts, shrink
  the range.

Progressions: bird dog 3 s → 5 s → elbow-to-knee under the body → standing single-leg version;
side plank knee → straight leg → top leg raised → top arm extended → dynamic hip drops; dead bug feet
down → feet up → both limbs → dumbbell.

### Rejected: Russian twist

Athlete asked directly. **No.** Three reasons, in order of weight:

1. **It trains active rotation and running is an anti-rotation sport.** The direction is exactly
   inverted.
2. **Its loading pattern is flexion + rotation + lumbar shear** — the combination most associated with
   disc stress — and it is usually done loaded and already fatigued.
3. **It is one of the most compensated movements there is.** In most people, "trunk rotation" is
   actually produced by the hips and shoulder girdle while the lumbar spine only flexes. It therefore
   frequently trains the wrong thing *and* loads the spine.

Anti-rotation replacement: bird dog, Pallof press (**requires an anchorable band** — a short loop band
cannot do it), single-arm plank, side plank. When no anchorable band exists, the working set is
dumbbell single-arm anti-rotation hold + single-arm plank + self-resistance rotation + wall push; see
`references/substitution-dimension-check.md` for why four movements are needed to cover one.

### The compensation rule (general)

> **Range and load obey position — never the reverse. If position is wrong, shrink the range; do not
> add load.**

Compensation almost always appears at the moment load or range is increased. Correct position with
small range always beats wrong position with large range.

Per-movement compensation → fix:

| Movement | Compensation | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Wall angel | lower back leaves the wall (lumbar extension substituting for thoracic) | brace the abdomen so the lower back stays flat; shrink range if it cannot |
| Prone Y-T-W | shoulder shrug (upper trap/neck substituting) | press shoulders **down and back** first, then lift |
| W isometric | chest thrust, ribs flared | ribs down, pelvis neutral |
| Fast arm swing | shrug; lateral arm sway | elbows in the vertical plane at the sides |
| Bird dog | pelvis rotates, lower back sags | glass of water on the back; leg no higher than the hip |
| Side plank | hip drops or tips forward | lift the hip, one straight line |
| Dead bug | lower back arches off the floor | hand under the back; shrink range the moment it lifts |

## 4b. What added mass actually does to the arm swing (2 kg ≠ strength work)

The athlete's own correction: *"2kg 它改变的是摆臂的惯性条件，而不是单纯增加肌肉力量。"*

At 2 kg with a ~0.4–0.5 m swing radius the added **moment of inertia** is meaningful, but 2 kg is far
too light to be a strength stimulus. What it changes:

1. the **inertial load** on the swing — the same angular velocity now needs more torque to accelerate
   and decelerate
2. the **neuromuscular conditions** for holding trajectory and timing under that inertia
3. **proprioceptive input** — "elbow in the vertical plane" is much easier to feel with mass in hand

So it is **control training under altered inertia**, not strengthening.

Two limits, both of which must be stated:

- **Do not run with the 2 kg.** It changes the real swing dynamics and may train a pattern that does
  not hold under normal conditions.
- **Its transfer is unproven.** There is no direct evidence that loaded standing arm swing carries over
  to running. **It is a tool, not a validated method.** Use it for slow, deliberate pattern work; get
  speed and endurance from speed and duration, not from weight.

### The anchoring concept for this whole module

Worth stating to the athlete once, because it is the actual reason the module exists:

> **Unnecessary tension is a substitute for control.** When you cannot hold a position, you grip
> everything to manufacture stability — clenched fists, shrugged shoulders, braced lumbar spine, held
> breath. So *reducing wasted tension* is not "relaxing"; it is **raising positional control**. That is
> the real connection between upper-body capacity and running economy.

It also produces the one mechanical fix that needs no mobility change: **are the hands clenched for the
whole run?** That is pure wasted tension and can be corrected directly.

## 5. Introducing a new movement

- **One at a time.** Two at once and you cannot tell which one did anything — good or bad.
- **Minimum dose on introduction** (1–2 sets, slow, unloaded), then watch 48 h for delayed soreness.
  Upper back is the most soreness-prone area and soreness attacks arm swing directly.
- **Prefer substitution over addition.** Unless there is a real uncovered gap, a new movement replaces
  an old one — otherwise 5 minutes becomes 15 and then stops happening.
- **The first two exposures are always unloaded, slow, and about finding the position.**
- **Judge after 4 weeks:** has the shoulder-sinking point moved later? No change → drop it rather than
  adding more.

---

## 6. Frequency and verification

Upper body 3×/week, core 2×/week. **Frequency is for adherence as much as for adaptation** — three
5-minute touches beat one 15-minute block, and the dose–response curve is flat: 1 → 3 sessions
matters, 3 → 6 barely does. Do not chase single-session volume.

**Verification needs no instrument:** *at what minute of a long run do the shoulders start to sink?*
60 → 75 minutes is a result. Check every 4 weeks. **No change means the dose or the movement selection
is wrong — adjust that, do not just add more.**

---

## 7. "Is this comprehensive enough?" — the answer is no, and that is correct

The question will be asked, and the temptation is to keep adding until the list looks complete.
**Do not.** A programme built for one specific problem is *complete for that problem*; it is not
supposed to be a comprehensive upper-body programme, because the extra coverage has no value for
running and costs the minutes that make the programme happen at all.

**The only legitimate way to reopen the question is: has the problem changed?**
Same symptom → the current set is enough, add nothing. A new symptom (a specific shoulder angle,
a back signal, an inability to hold the pelvis during single-leg work) → re-audit, and it may call for
entirely different movements.

State this to the athlete **before** they ask, because "comprehensive" is an appealing goal and the
failure mode (a 20-minute programme that never happens) is invisible until months later.
